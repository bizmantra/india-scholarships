# Study Abroad terms: keep the official name, explain it once

Ported from the retired repo's `STUDY_ABROAD_CONTENT_RULES.md` §4 and `src/lib/termDictionary.ts`. Rule: [CONTENT_RULES.md](CONTENT_RULES.md) §4. Define each term on its first use in a page.

The old dictionary also carried amounts (blocked account €11,904 and €992 a month, SEVIS $350, TCS 0.5% on education loans). **They are not repeated here.** Re-check any amount against the official source on the day you write, and cite it with `checked_at`.

| Official term | Plain-English line to put next to it |
|---|---|
| I-20 Form | The official admission paper sent by your US university, proving you are accepted. |
| Blocked Account (Sperrkonto) | A secure German bank account where you deposit your living funds to show the government you can support yourself. |
| APS Certificate | A document that proves your Indian school or college degree is genuine and recognised in Germany. |
| SEVIS Fee (I-901) | A mandatory US government fee to register you in their student tracking database. |
| DS-160 | The main online visa application form you must submit before your interview. |
| VFS Global | The official company that handles passport collection and biometrics (fingerprints) for the embassy. |
| Section 80E | An Indian tax rule that lets the person repaying an education loan deduct the loan interest from taxable income. |
| LRS (Liberalised Remittance Scheme) | The Reserve Bank of India rule that limits how much money an Indian resident can send abroad in a year. |
| TCS (Tax Collected at Source) | A tax collected when money is sent abroad, which can be claimed back when you file your tax return. |
| Collateral | An asset (house, gold, fixed deposit) pledged to a bank, usually to get a lower interest rate. |
| Co-signer / Co-borrower | The parent or guardian who signs the loan with you and takes responsibility for repayment. |

Note on Section 80E: the old rule said "100% of interest". Check the current Income Tax Act wording before claiming that.
