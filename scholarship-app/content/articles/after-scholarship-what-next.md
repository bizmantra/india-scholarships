## The scholarship is only half the loop

Most students stop paying attention once the application is submitted. In practice a scholarship has four stages, and each one can fail on its own: institute verification, the PFMS credit, the renewal, and the next academic year's application.

If you are still picking a scheme rather than waiting on one, start from the [scholarship categories hub](/scholarships-by-category) instead.

## 1. Confirm the money actually reached you

Selected is not the same as paid. After your institute verifies the application, the amount moves through PFMS before it reaches your bank account, and a payment can sit in one of several states for weeks.

The [PFMS payment status guide](/guides/pfms-scholarship-payment-status-tracking-guide) explains what each status on the portal means and which one actually needs you to visit the college office.

## 2. Renew while the window is open

Most central and state schemes are renewable for the full length of the course, but renewal is a fresh application with its own deadline — not an automatic continuation. Miss the window and you lose the year, even if your marks are fine.

The [National Scholarship Portal guide](/guides/nsp-national-scholarship-portal-guide) covers renewal dates, institute verification and how a renewal sits alongside a first-time application.

## 3. Replace the documents that expire

Income, caste and domicile certificates have validity windows, and an expired certificate is one of the most common reasons a renewal is rejected. Get the replacement before the portal opens rather than after the objection is raised.

The [state income and domicile certificate guide](/guides/state-income-domicile-certificate-guide) lists what each certificate needs and how long it stays valid.

## 4. Plan the government exam attempt

Once your degree is underway, the next serious deadline is usually an exam rather than another scholarship. Age is the one eligibility rule students check last — and the one that cannot be fixed later.

Limits below are the ones used in recent cycles. They change between cycles and between posts, so treat them as a guide and confirm in the notification for the year you apply.

| Exam | Minimum age | Upper limit — General / EWS | OBC-NCL (+3) | SC / ST (+5) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SSC CGL | 18 years (some posts 20) | up to 32 years, varies by post | up to 35 years | up to 37 years |
| UPSC Civil Services | 21 years | 32 years | 35 years | 37 years |
| IBPS PO / MT | 20 years | 30 years | 33 years | 35 years |

For SSC CGL the upper limit depends on the post: some posts cap at 27 or 30, so work out your limit from the post you want, not the headline 32.

Two rules trip up most candidates:

- **The cutoff date is fixed.** Your age on that single date decides the cycle. Crossing the upper limit after the cutoff does not make you ineligible for that cycle — it ends your eligibility for the next one.
- **Relaxation is on the upper limit only.** The minimum age does not move for any category, EWS gets no relaxation at all, and PwBD candidates get a further 10 years on top of their category limit.

Check your own date of birth rather than a rule of thumb — [the exam age-limit checkers](https://indiacalculators.org/exams/?utm_source=indiascholarships&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=guide) give the last year you can apply for SSC, UPSC and IBPS, and flag the post groups with a lower cap.

## 5. Keep the scholarship and the exam separate

Nothing about accepting a scholarship stops you from writing a government exam, and nothing about writing an exam affects a renewal. The only place the two meet is paperwork: an OBC-Non-Creamy-Layer certificate has a validity window for both the scholarship renewal and the exam application, so check the issue date once and replace it in one trip.

## What to do this week

- Check the PFMS status on your application and note anything stuck before the institute stage.
- Put the renewal window for your scheme in your calendar, not your memory.
- Photograph your income, caste and domicile certificates and note their expiry dates.
- If your degree is in its final year, check the age cutoff for the exams you plan to write.

Confirm every limit against the notification for the cycle you are applying to — age rules are revised between cycles, and the notification for your year is the only version that counts.
