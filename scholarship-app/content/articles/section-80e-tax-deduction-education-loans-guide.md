Section 80E of the Income Tax Act provides essential tax relief.

The provision allows full deduction of interest paid on student loans.

Tax deductions substantially reduce the net borrowing expense of higher studies.

## What Is Section 80E of the Income Tax Act?

Section 80E provides tax deductions exclusively on educational loan interest.

Taxpayers claim this deduction under Chapter VI-A during tax filing.

The benefit applies only under the Old Tax Regime framework.

The New Tax Regime under Section 115BAC does not allow this deduction.

Taxpayers must select the Old Tax Regime to claim interest deductions.

Tax savings apply directly against total gross taxable income.

---

## Core Statutory Rules Under Section 80E

Tax laws establish clear statutory boundaries for claiming education loan benefits.

Borrowers must understand three central rules governing Section 80E claims.

- **No Upper Ceiling on Interest**: Taxpayers can deduct the entire interest paid without rupee limits.
- **Eight-Year Deduction Cap**: Claims remain valid for eight consecutive assessment years maximum.
- **Interest Only Coverage**: Principal repayment does not qualify for tax deductions under Section 80E.

The eight-year window starts in the year interest payment begins.

The deduction terminates after eight years or upon loan payoff.

Interest payments made after eight years receive zero tax benefits.

Principal repayments cannot be claimed under Section 80C either.

Borrowers should service interest within the eight-year window to maximize total tax benefits.

---

## Approved Lenders: Banking Companies vs Notified Institutions

Tax regulations restrict eligible education loans to recognized institutions.

Loans from unauthorized lenders cannot yield tax deductions.

Statutory rules explicitly define eligible lenders into three categories:
- **Scheduled Commercial Banks**: Public, private, and foreign banks governed by the Banking Regulation Act.
- **Notified Financial Institutions**: Institutions explicitly notified by the Central Government in the Official Gazette.
- **Approved Charitable Institutions**: Educational trusts and charities recognized under Section 80G(2)(a).
- **Ineligible Lenders**: Loans from unapproved private lenders, friends, relatives, and non-notified platforms do not qualify.

Borrowers must verify that their sanction letter specifies an eligible banking institution.

## Who Can Claim Section 80E Deductions?

Tax laws restrict Section 80E claims to individual taxpayers.

The loan must be sanctioned in the taxpayer's name for designated beneficiaries:
- **Self**: Students earning taxable income can claim deductions for self-study loans.
- **Spouse**: An individual can claim deductions for loans funding a spouse.
- **Children**: Parents can claim deductions for education loans supporting their children.
- **Legal Guardian**: Legal guardians can claim interest deductions for dependent wards.

Each co-borrower claims deductions strictly for interest amounts paid from their taxable income.

The loan must support recognized post-secondary higher education programs.

Eligible courses include undergraduate, postgraduate, and professional degree studies.

Both domestic and international degree programs qualify for full deductions.

## Required Documentation Checklist

Claiming Section 80E requires strict documentary proof during tax filing.

Salaried employees must submit documentation to payroll finance teams.

Self-employed professionals maintain these records for tax audit verifications.

- **Annual Interest Certificate**: Lenders provide certificates separating principal and interest components.
- **Loan Sanction Letter**: The sanction document confirms approval for recognized higher studies.
- **Course Admission Proof**: University admission offers establish student enrollment in approved programs.
- **Bank Payment Records**: Bank statements prove electronic interest debits from taxable income.

Banks issue provisional certificates at the start of financial years.

Final interest certificates become available after the fiscal year ends.

## Step-by-Step Procedure to Claim Section 80E

Follow this sequential process to claim Section 80E tax deductions.

Proper submission prevents delays in tax deductions and refund processing.

Accurate claims lower overall income tax liabilities during assessment.

1. **Obtain Bank Certificate**: Download the annual loan interest certificate from internet banking.
2. **Submit Payroll Declaration**: Provide the certificate to the employer during tax declaration cycles.
3. **Select Old Regime**: Confirm selection of the Old Tax Regime for the year.
4. **Verify Form 16**: Ensure the payroll team reflects interest under Chapter VI-A deductions.
5. **File Income Tax Return**: Enter final interest paid under Section 80E on ITR forms.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid Under Section 80E

Tax filers frequently make errors that invalidate Section 80E claims.

Identifying these common pitfalls prevents tax notices and denied deductions.

Careful adherence to tax rules ensures uninterrupted tax savings.

- **New Tax Regime Selection**: Opting for the New Regime forfeits Section 80E claims completely.
- **Unapproved NBFC Borrowing**: Taking loans from non-notified fintech lenders prevents tax deductions.
- **Claiming Beyond Eight Years**: Interest payments past eight consecutive assessment years receive zero relief.
- **Claiming Principal Repayments**: The Income Tax Department disallows principal amounts because Section 80E covers interest only.

Wrongful principal claims risk disallowance and penalties for under-reporting income under Section 270A.

Maintaining clean financial documentation ensures smooth tax filing every year.
