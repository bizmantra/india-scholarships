Securing a German student visa requires proof of adequate financial resources.

The German government calls this proof a Finanzierungsnachweis.

International students satisfy this rule by opening an escrow account called a Sperrkonto.

---

## What Is a German Blocked Account?

A blocked account is a specialized bank account held in Germany.

The account holds living expense funds for an entire academic year.

Funds remain blocked until the student arrives legally in Germany.

Students withdraw a fixed statutory allowance each calendar month.

No student can withdraw the entire lump sum immediately.

This arrangement guarantees that foreign scholars can cover accommodation, food, and health insurance.

Students can compute total upfront costs using the [Blocked Account Calculator](/study-abroad/tools/blocked-account-calculator).

Explore full degree criteria in the [Study in Germany Guide](/study-abroad/study-in/germany).

## Statutory Legal Requirement Under AufenthG § 16b

Section 16b of the German Residence Act governs student residence permits.

This statute is officially cited as AufenthG § 16b.

The German Federal Foreign Office sets mandatory annual minimum deposit thresholds.

The current statutory minimum deposit is **€11,904 per year**.

This total equates to exactly **€992 per month** for twelve months.

Consulates reject visa files that show lower deposit balances.

Key regulatory principles governing the blocked deposit include:

- **Monthly Cap**: Payouts cannot exceed €992 per calendar month.
- **Deposit Buffer**: Providers collect an additional €100 buffer for incoming transfer deductions.
- **Primary Account Holder**: The account must remain in the student's legal name.
- **Sponsor Rules**: Third-party family members cannot open accounts in their own name.
- **Academic Validation**: Most students obtain verification through the [APS Certificate Guide](/guides/how-to-get-germany-aps-certificate-india-guide).

## Leading Approved Digital Providers: Expatrio, Fintiba, and Coracle

The German Federal Foreign Office accepts verified digital blocked account providers.

Three digital platforms dominate the international student market in India:

1. **Expatrio**: A digital platform partnered with European-regulated Aion Bank.
2. **Fintiba**: A digital specialist partnered with Hamburg-based Sutor Bank.
3. **Coracle**: An independent platform offering zero monthly maintenance fee packages.

Expatrio operates through Aion Bank with German International Bank Account Numbers.

Aion Bank provides statutory deposit protection up to €100,000 under European Union regulations.

The standard setup fee is €49 alongside a €5 monthly fee.

Students bundling public health insurance often receive a full €49 fee refund.

Account setup operates entirely online via digital smartphone verification.

### 2. Fintiba

Fintiba partners with Sutor Bank, an established private German financial institution.

Sutor Bank operates under direct supervision of the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin).

Fintiba Basic charges an initial setup fee of €89.

The platform levies an ongoing monthly maintenance fee of €4.90.

The Fintiba Plus bundle includes statutory health insurance and free travel insurance.

### 3. Coracle

Coracle offers flexible account structures designed for budget-conscious Indian students.

The platform charges an upfront enrollment fee of €59.

Coracle charges €0 in recurring monthly maintenance fees.

Enrolling in the Coracle Prime bundle waives the initial setup fee completely.

The bundle pairs the account with approved German statutory public health insurance.

## Step-by-Step Blocked Account Opening Procedure

Indian students can open a digital blocked account entirely online before securing visas.

Follow this sequence to create an account and generate official deposit credentials:

1. **Select an Approved Provider**: Choose Expatrio, Fintiba, or Coracle based on service costs.
2. **Submit Online Application**: Fill in personal contact details, permanent Indian address, and date of birth.
3. **Upload Identity Documents**: Submit a clear color scan of the valid Indian passport biographical page.
4. **Provide Academic Proof**: Upload the German university admission letter or university entrance confirmation document.
5. **Complete Identity Verification**: Perform automated facial scanning or brief video identification via the portal.
6. **Obtain Account Details**: Download the opening document containing dedicated German IBAN and SWIFT codes.

The dedicated IBAN belongs exclusively to the applicant.

Never remit funds to generic pooling accounts lacking personal account identification.

## Money Transfer from India: RBI LRS, Form A2 & TCS Rules

Transferring money from India requires compliance with Reserve Bank of India foreign exchange rules.

The Reserve Bank of India governs international remittances under the Liberalised Remittance Scheme.

Indian citizens can remit up to USD 250,000 per financial year under this framework.

Calculate applicable remittance tax using the [RBI LRS TCS Calculator](/study-abroad/tools/rbi-lrs-tcs-calculator).

Detailed regulatory thresholds appear in the comprehensive [RBI LRS Remittance Guide](/guides/rbi-lrs-tcs-rules-study-abroad-remittances-guide).

### Form A2 and Authorized Dealer Banks

Outward bank transfers require submitting a completed Form A2 application.

Students submit this document through an authorized dealer bank or digital platform.

Key documentation required for outward international wire transfers:

- **Form A2 Form**: Declaring remittance purpose code S0305 for educational living expenses.
- **LRS Declaration**: Confirming total foreign remittances remain within annual central limits.
- **Permanent Account Number**: Valid PAN card details of the remitter and student.
- **Bank Account Statement**: Financial statements proving origin of remitted funds.
- **Blocked Account Details**: Official account opening document displaying German IBAN and BIC.

### Tax Collected at Source (TCS) Rules

Tax Collected at Source applies to outward foreign remittances exceeding specified thresholds:

- **Threshold Limit**: No tax collection occurs on remittances up to ₹7,00,000 annually.
- **Remittances Above ₹7 Lakh via Loan**: A concessional 0.5% rate applies to educational loan disbursements.
- **Remittances Above ₹7 Lakh via Savings**: A 5% rate applies to personal bank savings.

Remitters can claim deducted tax back when filing annual Indian income tax returns.

Calculate educational loan repayment figures using the [Education Loan EMI Calculator](/tools/education-loan-emi-calculator).

## Blocking Confirmation (Sperrbestätigung) for Visa Appointments

International transfers typically settle within three to five business days.

Once funds arrive, the provider locks the statutory €11,904 balance.

The provider then issues an official certificate titled the **Sperrbestätigung**.

This certificate serves as legal proof of financial resources for German consulates.

Key guidelines for presenting the document:

- **Mandatory Submission**: Submit this document during the VFS Global or Embassy visa appointment.
- **Exact Matches**: Ensure passport names match the blocking certificate spelling exactly.
- **Full Deposit Proof**: The certificate must confirm receipt of the complete €11,904 sum.
- **Digital Authenticity**: Most providers include electronic verification codes and digital seals.

Visa officers reject applications supported only by Indian outward bank remittance slips.

Only the official Sperrbestätigung from the German account provider satisfies consular standards.

## Arrival in Germany: City Registration & Girokonto Unblocking

Money deposited in a blocked account cannot be withdrawn directly using an Indian card.

Students must complete three essential administrative tasks upon arriving in Germany:

### 1. Complete City Registration (Anmeldung)

Book an appointment at the local residents registration office (Bürgeramt or Einwohnermeldeamt).

Registration must occur within fourteen days of moving into local student housing.

Students present a signed landlord confirmation document called the Wohnungsgeberbestätigung.

The registration authority issues an official registration certificate known as the Meldebescheinigung.

Keep this document safe as German banks require it for identity verification.

### 2. Open a Local Current Account (Girokonto)

Open an active German checking account known locally as a Girokonto.

Popular retail and online banking institutions include:

- **Sparkasse**: Extensive branch network and student-friendly campus services across German cities.
- **Deutsche Bank**: Traditional banking institution offering broad English customer assistance.
- **Commerzbank**: Digital banking access alongside physical customer support branches.
- **N26**: Modern mobile bank providing rapid digital setup and free virtual debit cards.

The local bank issues a German IBAN for receiving regular monthly transfers.

### 3. Activate Monthly Blocked Payouts

Log into the digital blocked account dashboard via website or mobile app.

Upload the required post-arrival verification records:

1. **German Entry Visa**: Passport page showing the consular student visa and airport entry stamp.
2. **Meldebescheinigung**: Official city registration certificate confirming local German address.
3. **Girokonto Details**: Local German checking account IBAN for receiving funds.

Once verified, the provider releases the first monthly payout of **€992**.

Subsequent €992 payouts transfer automatically on a recurring monthly date throughout the year.

Funding a blocked account entirely from family savings is challenging for many Indian students.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid When Opening a Blocked Account

Navigating international financial systems requires strict attention to procedural details.

Avoid these frequent mistakes to prevent visa delays or frozen transfers:

- **Remitting Incomplete Amounts**: Always include intermediary bank transfer fees in remittance calculations.
- **Mismatched Remitter Information**: Enter student registration numbers correctly in SWIFT wire transfer reference fields.
- **Delaying Wire Transfers**: Initiate remittances at least four weeks before scheduled visa appointments.
- **Ignoring City Registration**: Complete registration immediately upon arrival to avoid delayed checking account activations.
- **Transferring from Unverified Accounts**: Remit funds exclusively from accounts maintained in the student or parent name.

Following these standardized procedures ensures uninterrupted financial compliance throughout German university studies.
