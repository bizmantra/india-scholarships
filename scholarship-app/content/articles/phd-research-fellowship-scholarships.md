## The Three Main PhD Funding Routes

PhD funding in India runs through three primary channels, and picking the right one depends on your subject area and target institute tier:

1. **CSIR-NET / UGC-NET JRF**: The standard national fellowship route — CSIR-NET for science subjects, UGC-NET for humanities, social sciences, and commerce.
2. **PMRF (Prime Minister's Research Fellowship)**: A high-value fellowship restricted to students entering PhD programs at IITs, IISc, NITs, and select IIITs, offering a substantially higher monthly stipend than standard NET-JRF.
3. **Institute/Category-Specific Fellowships**: University-specific research assistantships, plus dedicated overseas PhD schemes for SC/ST/OBC students.

> 💡 **Pro Tip**: If you're eligible for both NET-JRF and PMRF, apply for PMRF first — it offers a higher stipend, and you can fall back on NET-JRF if you aren't selected.

## Choosing the Right Fellowship

If your subject is a pure or applied science, CSIR-NET JRF is usually the fastest route to funded PhD registration. For social sciences, humanities, commerce, and management research, UGC-NET JRF is the equivalent pathway. Engineering PhD candidates at top institutes should specifically evaluate PMRF, since the stipend gap compared to standard JRF is substantial.

---

## Application Tips

1. **Apply for NET/PMRF eligibility before finalizing your PhD admission**, since some universities require proof of fellowship at the registration stage itself.
2. **Draft a strong research synopsis** — this is weighted heavily for PMRF and institute-specific assistantships.
3. **Check category-specific overseas schemes early** if you're considering a PhD abroad — application windows often open a year before your intended enrollment.
