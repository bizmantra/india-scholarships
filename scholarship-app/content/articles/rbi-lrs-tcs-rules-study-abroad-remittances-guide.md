Sending tuition fees or living expenses overseas requires understanding Indian banking rules.

The Reserve Bank of India governs all outward foreign currency remittances. Tax rules also mandate Tax Collected at Source on large transfers.

Understanding these regulations helps families save money and avoid unexpected tax deductions.

## What Is the RBI Liberalised Remittance Scheme?

The Liberalised Remittance Scheme is an official Reserve Bank of India framework.

It allows resident individuals to remit foreign currency abroad without prior central approval.

Key scheme provisions:
- **Annual Limit**: Resident individuals can remit up to **$250,000** per financial year (April to March).
- **Eligible Purposes**: Includes overseas education, university housing deposits, living allowances, and international travel.
- **Separate Quotas**: Both the student and funding parents hold separate $250,000 annual limits.

Transfers exceeding this limit require special authorization from the Reserve Bank of India.

## TCS Tax Rates on Overseas Education Transfers

Tax Collected at Source applies under Section 206C(1G) of the Income Tax Act.

The tax rate depends on how the educational remittance is funded:

| Funding Source | Remittance Up to ₹7,00,000 | Remittance Above ₹7,00,000 |
|---|---|---|
| **Education Loan from Approved Bank** | **0% (Nil)** | **0.5%** on the excess amount |
| **Self-Funded / Family Savings** | **0% (Nil)** | **5.0%** on the excess amount |
| **General Non-Education Foreign Outflow** | **0% (Nil)** | **20.0%** on the excess amount |

The ₹7,00,000 exemption threshold applies per remitter across the entire financial year.

## How to Qualify for the Lower 0.5% TCS Rate

Using an education loan provides significant tax benefits on foreign transfers.

To secure the preferential **0.5% TCS rate**, you must satisfy specific criteria:
1. **Approved Financial Institution**: The loan must originate from a recognized banking company under Section 80E.
2. **Direct Bank Disbursement**: The lending bank transfers tuition funds directly to the overseas university.
3. **Formal Sanction Letter**: The remitter submits an official loan sanction letter alongside Form A2.

Private personal loans or unapproved lenders do not qualify for the 0.5% concession.

## Step-by-Step Remittance Procedure

Follow this procedural sequence when transferring tuition fees or living funds:

1. **Obtain University Invoice**: Secure the official tuition fee payment notice showing university wire details.
2. **Complete Form A2**: Fill out the mandatory statutory foreign exchange declaration with your authorized bank.
3. **Submit LRS Declaration**: Confirm the cumulative foreign exchange purchased during the current financial year.
4. **Provide Education Proof**: Attach your university admission letter and verified student visa copy.
5. **Deduct Applicable TCS**: The authorized dealer bank calculates and deducts applicable TCS automatically.
6. **Obtain Swift Receipt**: Retain the generated SWIFT MT103 document confirming foreign wire transmission.

## How to Claim Your Full TCS Refund

Tax Collected at Source is not an ultimate financial expenditure.

It operates as advance income tax credited to the remitter's tax account.

Steps to reclaim deducted TCS:
- **Form 26AS Reflection**: The collecting bank deposits TCS under the remitter's PAN within quarterly tax filings.
- **Verify AIS Record**: Check that the collected tax appears on your Annual Information Statement.
- **File Income Tax Return**: Declare the deducted tax credits when filing annual income tax returns.
- **Offset Tax Liability**: Deduct the collected amount against total annual income tax obligations.
- **Direct Bank Refund**: If total tax liability is zero, the tax department refunds the full amount.
