Sending money abroad for education requires following Reserve Bank of India foreign exchange regulations.

The Reserve Bank governs outward remittances under the Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS).

Tax rules also mandate Tax Collected at Source (TCS) on foreign educational wire transfers.

Calculate exact tax and transfer deductions using the [RBI LRS TCS Calculator](/study-abroad/tools/rbi-lrs-tcs-calculator).

## What Is the RBI Liberalised Remittance Scheme?

The Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS) is an official Reserve Bank of India framework.

It allows Indian residents to remit foreign currency abroad without prior central bank approval.

An Authorized Dealer (AD Bank) is a financial institution licensed to process foreign exchange.

Key scheme rules:
- **Annual Limit**: Individuals can transfer up to **$250,000** per financial year from April to March.
- **Permitted Uses**: Includes university tuition, campus accommodation fees, living expenses, and health insurance.
- **Separate Allocations**: Both student and parents maintain separate $250,000 limits each fiscal year.

Transfers exceeding this annual ceiling require special authorization from the Reserve Bank.

## TCS Tax Rates on Overseas Education Transfers

Tax Collected at Source applies under Section 206C(1G) of the Income Tax Act.

TCS operates as an advance tax collection rather than an extra permanent cost.

Applicable tax percentages depend directly on how your educational remittance is funded:

| Funding Mechanism | Transfers Up to ₹7,00,000 | Transfers Above ₹7,00,000 |
|---|---|---|
| **Education Loan from Approved Bank** | **0% (Nil)** | **0.5%** on the excess amount |
| **Self-Funded / Family Savings** | **0% (Nil)** | **5.0%** on the excess amount |
| **Other Non-Education Transfers** | **0% (Nil)** | **20.0%** on the excess amount |

The standard **₹7,00,000 exemption threshold** applies across all transfers in one fiscal year.

## How to Secure the Concessional 0.5% TCS Rate

Funding education through a formal bank loan offers substantial tax benefits on remittances.

To secure the lower **0.5% TCS rate**, you must satisfy specific legal requirements:
1. **Approved Financial Institution**: Loans must come from a banking institution defined in Section 80E. Section 80E covers tax deductions on formal higher education loan interest.
2. **Education Purpose Verification**: The remittance form must explicitly state overseas education expenses.
3. **Formal Sanction Documentation**: Borrowers must submit a valid bank loan sanction certificate.

Personal loans or unapproved non-bank lenders do not qualify for the 0.5% concessional bracket.

## Step-by-Step Remittance Procedure

Follow this orderly workflow when transferring university tuition or maintenance funds abroad:

1. **Obtain University Invoice**: Request the official payment letter showing university wire details. An IBAN or account number identifies recipient university accounts.
2. **Complete Form A2**: Fill out Form A2, the statutory declaration for purchasing foreign exchange.
3. **Submit LRS Declaration**: Declare cumulative foreign exchange remittances made during the current fiscal year.
4. **Attach Educational Proof**: Provide your unconditional university admission letter and passport copy.
5. **Deduct Applicable TCS**: The authorized dealer bank calculates and collects statutory TCS automatically.
6. **Obtain SWIFT Confirmation**: Retain the SWIFT MT103 document. A SWIFT MT103 document proves your bank dispatched the foreign wire instruction.

## How to Claim Your Full TCS Credit or Refund

Deducted TCS is credited directly to the remitter's Permanent Account Number (PAN).

Form 26AS is the consolidated annual tax credit statement issued by the government.

The Annual Information Statement (AIS) also records reported outward foreign currency remittances.

Follow these steps to recover deducted TCS amounts:
- **Verify Form 26AS**: Confirm your remitting bank deposited TCS funds within quarterly returns.
- **Review AIS Records**: Verify the outward remittance amount appears correctly on your tax statement.
- **File Income Tax Return**: Report all collected TCS credits while submitting your annual return.
- **Offset Annual Taxes**: Adjust the collected tax credit against your total income tax liability.
- **Receive Direct Refund**: The Income Tax Department refunds any surplus credit directly to your account.
