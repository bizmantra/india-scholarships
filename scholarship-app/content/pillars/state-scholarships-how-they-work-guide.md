Every Indian state runs its own scholarship system, and if you've only ever looked at one state's portal, the others can feel confusing — different names, different websites, different rules. But underneath the differences, almost every state follows the same basic pattern. This guide explains that pattern, so you know what to look for no matter which state you're in.

## State vs Central: two completely separate systems

The first thing to understand is that **state scholarships and central scholarships are not the same system**.

- **State scholarships** are funded by your state government and applied for on that state's own portal. You must hold **domicile** (permanent residence) in that state to qualify.
- **Central scholarships** are funded by the national government or UGC/AICTE and applied for through the **National Scholarship Portal (NSP)** — open to eligible students across the country, regardless of which state they live in.

You can often qualify for both at the same time, since they're funded and run independently. See our [NSP guide](/pillars/nsp-national-scholarship-portal-guide) for how the central system works.

## The pattern that repeats across almost every state

Once you understand this pattern, every state's system becomes much easier to navigate — even if the portal names and exact rules differ.

**1. Split by caste category.** Most states run separate application forms for **SC**, **ST**, **OBC**, and **General/EWS** students. Some states combine categories (Uttar Pradesh, for example, combines General, SC, and ST into one form), while others keep every category fully separate. Never assume — check your specific state.

**2. Split by class stage.** Almost every state divides its main scholarships into **Pre-Matric** (usually Class 1-10) and **Post-Matric** (Class 11 onwards, including college). These are separate forms, and applying to the wrong stage for your current class is one of the most common — and most avoidable — mistakes.

**3. One main portal, plus extra department-specific schemes.** Most states run their core scholarships through one central website (like scholarship.odisha.gov.in or Karnataka's SSP portal), but almost every state also has smaller, separate schemes run by individual departments — for students with disabilities, specific subjects like Sanskrit, research fellowships, or children of ex-servicemen. These are easy to miss if you only check the main portal.

## Documents nearly every state asks for

1. **Aadhaar Card**, linked to your mobile number and bank account.
2. **State Domicile / Residence Certificate**, proving you live in that state.
3. **Family Income Certificate**, since most schemes cap eligibility by household income.
4. **Caste Certificate**, only if applying to a category-specific scheme.
5. **Latest marksheet**, matching your current class or stage.
6. **Bank passbook seeded with Aadhaar**, since most states pay directly to your account through DBT or PFMS.

## Why applications actually get rejected or delayed

It's rarely about not qualifying. The most common real-world issues are:

- **Wrong form for your class stage** — applying to Post-Matric when you're still in Pre-Matric range, or vice versa.
- **Expired or mismatched domicile/caste certificates** — many states require these to be recently issued, not just held from a previous year.
- **Bank account not seeded with Aadhaar** — payment fails even after an application is approved, since most states now pay through DBT/PFMS.

## How to actually use this guide

1. **Find your state's specific guide** from our [full state list](/state-scholarships) — the general pattern above applies everywhere, but the exact scheme names, portal, and category rules are state-specific.
2. **Confirm your category group and class stage** before you start filling any form — this single step avoids most of the common mistakes.
3. **Check for department-specific schemes** beyond the main portal — disability, subject-specific, and research schemes are easy to miss.
4. **Also check the central NSP system** — you may be eligible for a state scholarship and a central one at the same time.
5. **Keep your Aadhaar-bank link current** year-round, since payment failures are one of the most common reasons for delays across every state.
