If you're studying engineering, funding for your degree doesn't come from one place — it comes from four genuinely different sources, and most students only ever check one of them. This guide walks through all four, so you don't miss money you're actually eligible for.

## The four sources of engineering scholarship money

**1. Government / AICTE schemes.** The **AICTE Pragati Scholarship for Girl Students** is the largest dedicated engineering-specific scheme — for girls at AICTE-approved diploma or degree engineering colleges, limited to two girls per family. There isn't an equivalent boys-only scheme, since most other government funding for engineering runs through general category-based schemes instead (see below).

**2. State government schemes.** Most state Post-Matric and Pre-Matric scholarships explicitly cover engineering as one of several eligible courses, based on your category and income — not a separate "engineering scholarship" by name, but real funding that applies to you if you're an engineering student meeting the state's caste/income criteria. Check your [state's pillar guide](/state-scholarships) for the specific scheme names.

**3. Corporate CSR programs.** Companies with an engineering-heavy hiring pipeline often fund dedicated engineering scholarships — **L&T Build India Scholarship**, **Schaeffler India Hope Engineering Scholarship**, **Amazon Future Engineer Scholarship**, and **Tata Capital Pankh Scholarship** all fall here. These are merit-based, often smaller in seat count, and close faster than government schemes. See our [Corporate & Private Scholarships Guide](/pillars/corporate-private-scholarships-guide) for how to apply early and avoid missing the window.

**4. Research fellowships.** If you're pursuing an **M.Tech or PhD**, not an undergraduate BTech, the **Prime Minister's Research Fellowship (PMRF)** and **UGC Junior Research Fellowship (JRF)** pay considerably more than undergraduate awards and include a monthly stipend — but only apply to postgraduate research students.

## Check caste-based schemes too — they're not course-specific

If you're SC, ST, OBC, or a minority student, don't stop at "engineering scholarships." State and central caste-based schemes (like the **Top Class Education Scheme for SC Students**) apply to engineering students the same as any other stream, and they can usually be held alongside an engineering-specific award since they're funded independently. Our [SC/ST Scholarships guide](/pillars/sc-st-scholarships-india-guide) covers this in full.

## What documents do engineering scholarships usually ask for?

1. **Aadhaar Card**, linked to your bank account.
2. **Admission or bonafide certificate** confirming you're enrolled at an AICTE-approved or state-recognized institution.
3. **Family Income Certificate** — required for almost all state and AICTE schemes.
4. **Caste Certificate**, only if applying to a category-specific scheme.
5. **Class 12 marksheet** for undergraduate applications, or your degree marksheet for postgraduate/research schemes.
6. **Bank passbook seeded with Aadhaar**, since most schemes pay directly to your account.

## How to actually approach this

1. **Check your state's scheme first** — it's usually the largest amount you're eligible for, based on category and income, not merit alone.
2. **If you're a girl student, check AICTE Pragati specifically** — it's one of the few large schemes built for engineering students by name.
3. **Apply to 2-3 corporate schemes in parallel**, since seats are limited and close quickly — don't wait for one result before applying to the next.
4. **If you're pursuing M.Tech/PhD, prioritize PMRF or UGC JRF** over undergraduate-style awards — they pay substantially more.
5. **Don't skip caste-based schemes** just because they're not labeled "engineering" — they apply to you if you qualify by category and income.
