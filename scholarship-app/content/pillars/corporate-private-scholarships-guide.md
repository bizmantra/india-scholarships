Corporate and private scholarships work differently from the government schemes most of this site covers. This guide explains what that difference actually means for you, how to tell a real scheme from a scam, and how to use both types together.

## Where does the money come from?

**Corporate scholarships** are funded by a company, usually out of its CSR (Corporate Social Responsibility) budget — a portion of company profit that Indian law requires large companies to spend on social causes, and education is one of the most common choices. **HDFC Bank Parivartan's ECSS Programme**, **Tata Capital Pankh Scholarship**, **Kotak Kanya Scholarship**, and **Generation Google Scholarship** are all examples of this — the company itself decides the eligibility rules and funds the award directly.

**Private scholarships** come from a foundation, trust, or NGO that isn't a company's CSR arm. **Sitaram Jindal Foundation Scholarship**, **LIC Golden Jubilee Scholarship**, and **Reliance Foundation Undergraduate Scholarship** fall in this category — some are set up by wealthy individuals or families, some by university endowments, and some by independent charitable trusts.

For a student, the practical difference is small — both are separate from government scholarships, both usually require an online application with documents, and both pay merit-and-need based awards. The main thing worth knowing is *who* is funding the scheme, since that tells you where to verify it's genuine.

## How this is different from a government scholarship

- **Government scholarships** (like state Post-Matric schemes or NSP-linked central schemes) usually run through one official portal per state or department, stay open for a longer window, and are funded from the public budget.
- **Corporate and private scholarships** are run independently by each company or foundation, often through their own website or a partner NGO, and stay open only until a fixed number of seats is filled — sometimes just a few weeks.

Because of this, corporate and private scholarships reward students who apply *early* in the cycle, not just students who apply correctly. Government scholarships are more forgiving on timing within their open window.

## How to tell a legitimate scheme from a scam

This matters more here than for government schemes, because there's no single official portal to check against. Before applying to any corporate or private scholarship:

1. **Confirm it's listed on the company or foundation's own official website** — not just a listicle or a third-party blog repeating the name.
2. **Never pay any fee.** No legitimate corporate or private scholarship charges you to apply, to "process" your application, or to release your award.
3. **Check for a real, named funder.** A registered company (like **IndianOil**, **GAIL**, **L&T**) or a known foundation/trust should be named clearly — vague names with no verifiable organisation behind them are a red flag.

## Can you hold a government and a corporate/private scholarship together?

Usually, yes. Government and corporate/private scholarships run through completely separate systems, so there's rarely a rule against holding both. A few individual schemes do ask you to declare other financial aid you're receiving — always check the specific scheme's terms rather than assuming.

## What documents do you typically need?

Corporate and private scholarships ask for mostly the same documents as government ones, plus sometimes an extra step:

1. **Aadhaar Card**, usually linked to your bank account.
2. **Family Income Certificate** — most schemes cap eligibility by household income, similar to government schemes.
3. **Latest marksheet** — Class 10, Class 12, or your most recent college semester result, depending on the scheme's eligibility level.
4. **College admission or bonafide certificate.**
5. **Bank passbook copy**, with Aadhaar-NPCI seeding, since payment usually goes directly to your account.
6. **A short essay or statement of purpose** — this is the one thing government schemes almost never ask for, but many corporate and private ones do, especially higher-value awards like **Adobe India's AI Research Fellowship** or **L'Oréal India's FYWIS Scholarship**.

## How to actually use this category

1. **Check both hubs, not just one.** Corporate and private scholarships often target different things — some corporate schemes focus on a specific field (engineering, sports, STEM), while private/foundation ones are more often general merit-cum-need.
2. **Apply as early in the window as you can** — unlike government schemes, seats can fill before the stated deadline.
3. **Keep your documents ready year-round.** Since these schemes can open and close quickly, having your income certificate and marksheets ready in advance matters more here than for government scholarships.
4. **Don't rule out combining this with a government scholarship** — most students can hold both.
