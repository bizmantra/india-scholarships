If you're an OBC student, your scholarship options split across two systems — central and state — and the terminology itself can be confusing since different states use different names for overlapping groups. This guide explains both, in plain language.

## Central vs state: two separate systems for OBC students

- **Central OBC schemes** are funded by the national government and applied for through the [National Scholarship Portal (NSP)](/pillars/nsp-national-scholarship-portal-guide). The largest is **PM-YASASVI**, which covers Pre-Matric and Post-Matric support, plus a separate **Top Class Education Scheme for OBC/EBC/DNT** students at premier institutions.
- **State OBC schemes** are funded by your own state government and applied for on that state's own portal — separate from NSP, and only open to residents (domicile) of that state.

Most students should check both, since they're funded independently and can often be held together.

## OBC, SEBC, BC, EBC — same idea, different names

This is where most confusion comes from. Different states use different terms for the same or overlapping backward-class categories:

- **OBC (Other Backward Classes)** — the national term, used by most states and the central government.
- **SEBC (Socially and Educationally Backward Class)** — used heavily in Odisha's own scheme names, often grouped with OBC in practice.
- **BC (Backward Classes)** — used by several states, sometimes split further into sub-categories like BC-A or BC-B.
- **EBC (Extremely Backward Classes)** — a distinct, more specific category, notably used in Bihar, separate from general OBC.

**What this means for you:** Don't assume a term you've seen in one state applies the same way in another. Always check your specific state's eligibility list.

## Documents OBC scholarships almost always ask for

1. **Aadhaar Card**, linked to your bank account.
2. **OBC / SEBC / BC Caste Certificate** — confirm the exact certificate type your state or scheme accepts.
3. **Non-Creamy Layer Certificate** — many schemes require this in addition to your caste certificate, to confirm your family doesn't exceed an income/status threshold.
4. **Family Income Certificate.**
5. **State Domicile / Residence Certificate**, for any state-run scheme.
6. **Latest marksheet**, matching your current class or stage.
7. **Bank passbook seeded with Aadhaar.**

## Which states have dedicated OBC schemes worth checking

Most states run a named OBC, SEBC, or BC/EBC Post-Matric scheme alongside the central PM-YASASVI program — including Maharashtra, Karnataka, West Bengal, Telangana, Gujarat, Bihar, and Kerala. If your state has a [dedicated pillar guide](/state-scholarships), check it for the exact scheme name and portal.

## How to actually approach this

1. **Check PM-YASASVI first** — it's the largest central program and applies regardless of which state you live in, as long as you meet the income and category criteria.
2. **Then check your state's own OBC/SEBC/BC scheme** — this is usually a separate application on a separate portal, and can often be held alongside the central scheme.
3. **Confirm whether you need a Non-Creamy Layer certificate**, not just a caste certificate — this trips up many otherwise-eligible applicants.
4. **Don't assume terminology carries over between states** — check the exact category name your state uses before assuming you don't qualify.
5. **If you also meet an income-based General/EWS scheme's criteria, check that too** — OBC status doesn't exclude you from unrelated income-based schemes.
