If you are an SC (Scheduled Caste) or ST (Scheduled Tribe) student, you may qualify for more scholarship money than you think. The problem is not that scholarships don't exist. The problem is that nobody explains the whole system in one place.

This guide keeps it simple. No hard words. No confusing government language. You will learn which type of scholarship to look for, what papers you need, and why most applications get rejected.

## The two types of SC/ST scholarships

Most students don't know this: **there is not one SC/ST scholarship. There are two separate systems.**

### 1. Central Government scholarships

This comes from the national government, for the whole country.

- For [SC students](/scholarships-for/sc), it comes from the Ministry of Social Justice & Empowerment.
- For [ST students](/scholarships-for/st), it comes from the Ministry of Tribal Affairs.

You apply on one website for the whole country: the **National Scholarship Portal**, or **NSP** (see our [complete NSP guide](/pillars/nsp-national-scholarship-portal-guide) for which schemes use it and why applications get stuck). The rules are the same in every state.

### 2. State Government scholarships

Your own state also runs its own scholarship program. This is separate money. It has a separate form, on a separate website.

Some examples:
- [Odisha](/scholarships-in/odisha) runs its post-matric scholarship on the scholarship.odisha.gov.in portal — see our [Odisha scholarships guide](/pillars/odisha-scholarships-guide).
- [West Bengal](/scholarships-in/west-bengal) runs **Oasis**, for SC/ST/OBC students — see our [West Bengal scholarships guide](/pillars/west-bengal-scholarships-guide).
- [Karnataka](/scholarships-in/karnataka) runs **SSP**, the State Scholarship Portal — see our [Karnataka scholarships guide](/pillars/karnataka-scholarships-guide).
- [Madhya Pradesh](/scholarships-in/madhya-pradesh) runs **MPTAAS** — see our [Madhya Pradesh scholarships guide](/pillars/madhya-pradesh-scholarships-guide).
- [Rajasthan](/scholarships-in/rajasthan) runs the **SJE** department scholarships — see our [Rajasthan scholarships guide](/pillars/rajasthan-scholarships-guide).
- [Chhattisgarh](/scholarships-in/chhattisgarh) runs its own Post-Matric portal — see our [Chhattisgarh scholarships guide](/pillars/chhattisgarh-scholarships-guide).

To apply for a state scholarship, you must usually prove you live in that state. This is called **domicile**. Without domicile, you usually cannot apply, even if you study there.

### Why this matters to you

You may see two schemes that sound like "the same" scholarship — one Central, one State. They are not the same. They have different websites, different forms, and different payment dates. A generic-sounding name like "Post-Matric Scholarship for SC Students" almost always means one specific state's scheme, not a national one.

## SC and ST are usually two different scholarships

Many students search for "SC ST scholarship" as if it is one thing. In most states, it is not. SC and ST are usually **two separate schemes**, with two separate budgets. This is true even when the rules look almost the same.

Here is what is usually different between the SC version and the ST version:

- **Income limit** — A common number is ₹2.5 lakh a year. Some states raise this limit for ST students in tribal areas. A few remove it completely.
- **Which certificate you need** — SC and ST certificates usually come from different offices. An ST certificate often needs proof from your community records. A certificate that works for SC will usually not work for ST.
- **Whether the scheme exists at all** — Not every state offers a strong scheme for both categories. One state may have a good SC scheme, but only a small ST scheme. Another state can be the opposite.
- **Your caste's status can change by state** — This is the most confusing part. Each state has its own official list of SC and ST communities, under Indian law. A community can be SC or ST in one state, but not in another. Your eligibility is based on your **home state's** list, not the state where you study.

**What this means for you:** Always open the exact page for your category (SC or ST) and your state. Do not trust a general listing that just says "SC/ST" — it may only show you one of the two.

## Documents you will almost always need

These documents come up again and again, no matter which scheme you apply for. Get them ready before you start any form.

1. **Caste Certificate** — from your local Tehsildar or Revenue Officer. Many states make you renew this every academic year. An old certificate is a top reason for rejection.
2. **Income Certificate** — for the current financial year, not an old one.
3. **Domicile / Residence Certificate** — needed for almost all State scholarships. Usually not needed for Central ones.
4. **Aadhaar Card linked to your bank account** — most scholarships pay money straight to your bank account. This is called DBT. A wrong link can delay or fail your payment.
5. **Last year's marksheet** and a **bonafide certificate** from your current school or college.

### The #1 reason applications get rejected

It is not low marks. It is not income. The biggest reason is a **caste certificate problem** — wrong office, or not renewed for this year. Check your certificate is current before you apply.

## How to find the right scheme — step by step

1. **Start with your home state**, not the state where you study. Most state schemes need domicile in that state.
2. **Check if your state has separate SC and ST schemes.** Open the exact page for your category.
3. **Check if you can apply for both Central and State.** Rules are different in every state.
4. **Check the income limit for your exact scheme.** Do not assume ₹2.5 lakh applies everywhere.
5. **Prepare your documents early**, especially your caste certificate.
